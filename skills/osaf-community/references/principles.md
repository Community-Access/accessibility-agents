# Community principles and resources

Part of the `osaf-community` skill. Read this before drafting anything another
person will read: a comment, an update, a recognition note.

The framework's Community phase points to the Accessibility Toolkit's
[Community Accessibility Resources](https://github.com/open-source-accessibility/accessibility-toolkit/blob/main/resources/community/community.md).
Its principles and resource lists are adapted here. The toolkit numbers the
Community actions in an older order, so its resources are matched to actions by
title below. The toolkit says its resources are examples, not framework
requirements or endorsements.

## Principles for every action

- **Treat accessibility as the baseline and usability as the goal.** Technical
  conformance is essential, but people must also be able to participate
  successfully, efficiently, and with dignity.
- **Include disabled people in decisions that affect them.** Lived experience
  and accessibility expertise complement standards and technical testing.
- **Provide multiple ways to participate.** Offer synchronous and asynchronous
  options when practical.
- **Respect privacy, autonomy, and labor.** Request consent before sharing
  personal information or recognizing a contributor, and do not assume
  substantial accessibility work will be unpaid.
- **Avoid tokenism.** No individual represents an entire disability community;
  seek perspectives that reflect the project's users.
- **Close the feedback loop.** Communicate decisions, outcomes, unresolved
  barriers, workarounds, and next steps.
- **Create paths to leadership.** Enable accessibility contributors to become
  reviewers, maintainers, advisors, and project leaders.

## Resources, by action

Action 1, invite community help (the toolkit's "Learning and participation"):

- [W3C Web Accessibility Initiative](https://www.w3.org/WAI/)
- [The A11Y Project](https://www.a11yproject.com/)
- [Community Access](https://community-access.org/) and its
  [GIT Going with GitHub](https://community-access.org/git-going-with-github/) course
- [Open Source Guides: How to Contribute](https://opensource.guide/how-to-contribute/)

Action 2, respond constructively (the toolkit's "Responding to accessibility
reports"):

- [Accessibility Toolkit ACCESSIBILITY.md, How we respond](https://github.com/open-source-accessibility/accessibility-toolkit/blob/main/ACCESSIBILITY.md#how-we-respond)
- [GitHub: templates for useful issues and pull requests](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/using-templates-to-encourage-useful-issues-and-pull-requests)
- [GitHub Accessibility documentation](https://accessibility.github.com/documentation)

Action 3, accessible ways to contribute (the toolkit's "Accessible community
practices"):

- [W3C: Involving Users in Web Projects](https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/involving-users/)
- [W3C: How People with Disabilities Use the Web](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/)
- [Open Source Guides: Building Welcoming Communities](https://opensource.guide/building-community/)
- [GitHub: adding a code of conduct](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/setting-up-your-project-for-healthy-contributions/adding-a-code-of-conduct-to-your-project)
- CHAOSS metrics for
  [project access](https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-project-access/),
  [event accessibility](https://chaoss.community/kb/metric-event-accessibility/) and
  [chat platform inclusivity](https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-chat-platform-inclusivity/)

Action 4, recognize contributions (the toolkit's "Recognition and community
health"):

- [Open Source Accessibility shoutout discussions](https://github.com/orgs/open-source-accessibility/discussions/new?category=shoutout)

  [contribution attribution](https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-contribution-attribution/) and
  [types of contributions](https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-types-of-contributions/)

- [Open Source Accessibility community discussions](https://github.com/orgs/open-source-accessibility/discussions)
- [Open Source Guides: Best Practices for Maintainers](https://opensource.guide/best-practices/)
